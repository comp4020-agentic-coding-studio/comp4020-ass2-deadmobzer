Theory without demonstration is just a claim, and claims are cheap. This
week takes the remote-association toolkit from week 2 and applies it, badly,
on purpose, to whatever object is closest to hand, so you can see exactly
where the vagueness is doing the work.

The worked example, for anyone who wants to see it done properly before we
do it badly: Ralph Lauren — born Ralph Lifshitz, no formal design training,
no inherited pedigree to speak of — built an entire aspirational old-money
lifestyle out of a polo shirt. The shirt was the random object on the desk.
Everything else was remote association, run all the way to fashion
dominance. Nobody in this room is being asked to do that. Just the small
version, live, in fifty minutes.

## Outline

- picking a problem specific enough to sound serious, vague enough to survive
  questions
- narrating the abstraction as it happens, not after
- the Ralph Lauren case study: one object, one invented pedigree, one empire
- the tell that separates a bullshitted answer from an honest "I don't know"
- setting up next week's pivot to tools and resources
