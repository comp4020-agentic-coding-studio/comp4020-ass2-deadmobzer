Pennycook and colleagues found that syntactically coherent nonsense, dressed
in the right vocabulary, reliably reads as profound to people who haven't
been warned to look closely. "Fake deep" is that finding turned into a
technique: this week names the grammar instead of just using it.

## Outline

- the grammar of sounding profound: rhetorical questions, false binaries,
  paragraphs that end on a fragment
- critique as a genre that borrows philosophy's costume without its rigour
- telling apart a real insight and a well-dressed non-sequitur, from the
  audience's seat
- why this needs a second week to actually stick
