Bring the paragraph that survived week 9's clinic, now paired against
something a real philosopher actually argued. This is where the comparison
from lecture gets run for a mark instead of an audience.

## In the session

- short pairings read side by side, no commentary first
- the room votes on whether the pairing holds up or quietly falls apart
- notes on what "it's satire" is doing when it's used as a defence here

## Afterwards

Same split as always: half for showing up, half for whether the pairing was
made honestly rather than rigged to flatter itself.
