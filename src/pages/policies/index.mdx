# Policies and support

## Brief timing

No brief in this course is released more than a few minutes before the
session it belongs to. This is university policy, not an oversight: Parkinson's
law holds that work expands to fill the time available for it, so the course
removes the time. A brief released early is a brief overthought. It also
happens to be authentic assessment — a real client sends the brief at 4:55pm
on a Friday, and the earlier you get comfortable with that, the better client
you become.

## Late work

There isn't a late-work form, because there isn't a deadline extension that
improves a bullshit answer. If you missed a tutorial, you missed the mark
attached to it; email your tutor if the reason involves anyone other than
you and they'll sort out what's fair.

## Extensions

Available for the usual reasons, on the usual university terms, through the
usual university process. Nothing about this course changes how extensions
work elsewhere; ask early, ask your convenor, and don't wait for the brief to
show up before you flag that you'll need one.

## Academic integrity

Submit your own bullshit. Presenting someone else's bullshit as your own is
still plagiarism, no matter how confidently it's delivered — the course marks
the performance of thinking on your feet, and that performance has to be
yours. Using an LLM to help you generate an answer is explicitly in scope
for weeks 4 and 5; passing off an LLM's answer as an oral performance you
could actually give live is not, and the midsem and final orals are built to
notice the difference.

## The one rule that isn't a bit

The number one thing is to follow the rubric and brief in full.

## Getting help

Office hours are listed on each tutor's [profile](/people/). If something in
a brief is genuinely unclear — as opposed to deliberately open-ended, which is
most of it — ask before the session it's due in, not after.
